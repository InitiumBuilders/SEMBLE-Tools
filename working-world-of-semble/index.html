Foundation

Eight axioms
before scale.

If the model ever grows to millions—or imagined worlds within worlds—these rules keep power legible and human agency intact.

AXIOM 01

The map is not the territory.

Models guide attention. They do not become evidence merely because they are elegant, emotionally powerful, or widely shared.

AXIOM 02

People are participants, not property.

No steward, provider, platform, or “world owner” owns a human being. Membership is consentful and revocable.

AXIOM 03

Agency before alignment.

Coordination should increase the ability to choose, speak, leave, fork, and disagree—not quietly remove those options.

AXIOM 04

Trust is plural.

Replace one opaque reputation score with visible dimensions: care, clarity, reliability, reciprocity, transparency, and respect for agency.

AXIOM 05

Layers may nest. Accountability cannot disappear.

A chain of providers or stewards must never become a chain of excuses. Every layer needs ownership of decisions and an appeal path.

AXIOM 06

Signal must stay honest.

First movers test reality. They are not an army for manufactured hype, fake reviews, coercive growth, or artificial consensus.

AXIOM 07

Stories are infrastructure.

Stories coordinate attention and meaning. Treat narrative power with the same care as code, money, identity, and compute.

AXIOM 08

Powerful AI must remain accountable to people.

Systems that recommend, rank, moderate, transact, or act need declared scope, traceable decisions, independent evaluation, incident records, and meaningful human recourse.

This is the core Semble progression. Authority is earned through contribution and stewardship, not granted by claiming ownership of a world.

A network of federations exchanging signal, stories, resources, and standards without requiring a single ultimate controller.

Goal → coordination without capture

Rule: every Semble states what it is for—and what it is not.

Rule: builders expose assumptions and invite testing.

Rule: stewardship is reviewable, revocable, and bounded.

The public surface: live builds, decisions, demos, stories, retrospectives, and accountable visibility.

Rule: attention cannot substitute for evidence.
FM

First Movers

A standing corps of respectful early users who test products, experiences, and decisions before scale.

Rule: signal is purchased; praise is not.
∞

Semble Together

A federation: multiple communities coordinating on shared protocols, pooled resources, or common missions.

Rule: federation must preserve local voice and exit.
↔

Provider / Relay

Any layer supplying infrastructure, compute, identity, distribution, discovery, or connectivity to others.

Rule: dependencies should be visible and portable where possible.
GG

Gratus Guide

An independent trust witness who verifies claims, reviews stewardship evidence, mentors stewards, and helps participants challenge or appeal decisions.

Rule: guides may inspect and attest; they do not own the people or systems they review.
SP

Same Page Operator

A coordination specialist who makes shared intent, definitions, commitments, decisions, disagreements, and next moves legible to everyone affected.

Rule: alignment is documented, never presumed.
AG

AI Gratus Guide

A specialized verifier for AI systems: evaluates declared purpose, limitations, test evidence, deployment controls, incidents, and human recourse.

Rule: AI may assist evaluation; contested human-impact decisions require accountable human review.

Gratus Protocol

Trust needs
witnesses.

Gratus becomes Semble’s accountability and verification layer. Same Page remains the alignment layer. They collaborate—but neither is allowed to become the other.

Verification · Accountability

Gratus Guides

Trusted reviewers who examine evidence, verify public claims, mentor stewards, surface conflicts of interest, witness corrections, and help participants navigate appeals.

Authority boundary: may verify, challenge, publish findings, and recommend correction. They do not secretly rank everyone, seize stewardship, or become an unappealable court.
Alignment · Coordination

Same Page Operators

Facilitators who keep intent, definitions, decisions, responsibilities, disagreements, and next moves visible across a Semble or Together.

Authority boundary: may clarify and document. They cannot manufacture consensus or rewrite dissent into agreement.
Safety · Incident response

Gratus Guardians

A narrow emergency specialization for protecting people and systems when a material safety or integrity incident is active. Their interventions are temporary, logged, and reviewable.

Authority boundary: emergency powers expire. Guardians cannot quietly convert temporary safety controls into permanent governance.

Gratus Receipt

Evidence instead of a magic trust score.

Every meaningful verification produces a portable receipt: what was reviewed, what evidence was considered, who reviewed it, what conflicts existed, what remained uncertain, when the finding expires, and how it can be challenged. Honor is behavior—not a badge.

Core rule: trust may be summarized, but the evidence trail must remain inspectable. No permanent universal score for a human being.
ScopeExactly what claim, steward, policy, or system was reviewed.
EvidenceSources, samples, test methods, and provenance.
ReviewerGuide identity, qualification, and independence.
ConflictsFunding, relationships, incentives, recusals.
FindingVerified, disputed, incomplete, corrected, or expired.
RecourseAppeal path, minority report, and correction history.
Platform example: a Google-like or other large “Together” could expose a Gratus layer independent from its product and growth functions so communities can review stewardship, policy execution, and AI behavior. This is a design proposal—not a claim about Google, OpenAI, or any company’s actual hidden structure.
AI Accountability Layer

Powerful AI needs
traceable stewardship.

If AI systems become builders, moderators, recommenders, operators, or agents inside a Semble, accountability cannot dissolve into “the model did it.” The provider, deployer, steward, verifier, and affected human each need a visible role.

Gratus AI Assurance

An accountability loop for AI systems.

The goal is not to pretend every model is perfectly explainable. The goal is to make responsibility, evidence, limitations, deployment controls, and recourse legible enough that power can be challenged and improved.

01
Declare purpose & boundaryWhat may the system do, for whom, with what data, and what is explicitly out of scope?
02
Publish capability & limitation evidenceVersioned evals, failure cases, uncertainty, known blind spots, and conditions where human review is required.
03
Red-team before privilegeThe more consequential the action, the stronger the adversarial testing and permission boundary before deployment.
04
Log material actionsKeep enough provenance to reconstruct consequential decisions without turning surveillance into the default.
05
Watch incidents in productionRecord failures, near misses, abuse patterns, reversals, mitigations, and model/version changes.
06
Guarantee human recourseAffected people can challenge material outcomes, reach a responsible human, and receive correction when warranted.
07
Re-verify after changeMajor model, data, policy, autonomy, or deployment changes invalidate stale assurances.
Battle-tested boundaries

Never let the verifier become the new ruler.

An accountability layer creates power of its own. Gratus therefore needs structural limits from day one.

Two-key reviewNo single guide both investigates and makes the final contested determination.
Conflict recusalA verifier with meaningful financial or relational conflicts steps out.
Rotating panelsHigh-impact systems are not permanently reviewed by the same closed circle.
Minority reportsDissenting reviewers may publish a reasoned alternate finding.
Evidence expiryTrust receipts expire when systems, policies, or material conditions change.
No pay-for-praisePayment can fund rigorous review, never a guaranteed positive finding.
Privacy floorAuditability does not justify collecting everything about everyone.
Appeal beyond providerWhere stakes are high, recourse cannot terminate with the same operator that made the disputed decision.
AI-specific rule: an AI system can help inspect evidence, run evals, summarize incidents, or detect inconsistencies—but it should not be the sole final authority over a contested decision materially affecting a person.

Failure Modes

What could
go wrong.

The strongest version of Semble is defined as much by what it prevents as what it enables.

Provider capture

Control risk

Infrastructure becomes so central that participants cannot realistically leave or negotiate.

Countermove: portability, federation, transparent dependencies, exit tests.

Manufactured consensus

Signal risk

Paid testers or coordinated groups become fake proof that “everyone agrees” or loves a product.

Countermove: pay for testing, never praise; publish methodology and disagreement.

Reputation tyranny

Trust risk

A single trust score becomes destiny, erasing context, growth, minority views, or second chances.

Countermove: plural dimensions, recency, evidence, appeals, no universal human score.

Story capture

Attention risk

Whoever controls the stage can define reality by controlling what is seen, repeated, rewarded, or ignored.

Countermove: provenance, context, plural stages, friction for amplification.

Nested accountability collapse

Governance risk

Every operator claims they were “just following the layer above,” leaving no one responsible.

Countermove: decision logs, named accountable stewards, local refusal rights.

Epistemic drift

Reality risk

A useful metaphor or simulation hypothesis gets treated as verified hidden infrastructure without evidence.

Countermove: explicit fact/model/story labels and reality checks built into the interface.

Verifier capture

Accountability risk

Guides become dependent on the very stewards or providers they are supposed to review, turning verification into theater.

Countermove: conflict disclosure, reviewer rotation, mixed funding, random assignment, recusal, and public receipts.

AI responsibility laundering

AI governance risk

Providers blame models, deployers blame providers, and operators blame automation until nobody owns the outcome.

Countermove: responsibility maps, versioned evals, incident logs, named deployment owners, and human appeal.

First Movers Sprint

Your first 25–100 real humans.

A builder brings a product. First Movers use it in context, complete structured missions, surface breakpoints, record friction, and return qualitative + quantitative signal. The outcome is not “100 fans.” The outcome is 100 informed interactions with reality.

Semble rule: sell honest use, structured feedback, and repeatable human signal. Never sell fake traction.

Mission

What Semble
could help solve.

These are the highest-leverage problems your vocabulary naturally points toward.

01 · COORDINATION

Help strangers become a team without becoming a mob.

Shared moves, explicit roles, reversible commitments, and visible disagreement.

02 · TRUST

Make trust legible without turning it into social credit.

Evidence-rich profiles with plural signals, appeals, provenance, and context.

03 · BUILDERS

Give builders real humans before they have distribution.

First Movers becomes infrastructure for testing, learning, and early community.

04 · GOVERNANCE

Make stewardship an earned practice instead of a throne.

Term limits, transparent decisions, conflict-of-interest disclosure, and exit rights.

05 · STORIES

Keep narrative power from silently becoming control.

Separate observation from interpretation, and interpretation from myth or worldbuilding.

06 · SCALE

Federate without flattening.

Let many sembles coordinate while preserving local variation, autonomy, and voice.

07 · ACCOUNTABILITY

Create independent witnesses for powerful systems.

Gratus Guides make claims, stewardship, corrections, and appeals legible without becoming a universal authority.

08 · AI

Keep responsibility attached to AI-mediated action.

AI assurance links model behavior back to evidence, deployment owners, incidents, safeguards, and human recourse.
